Who Are UofL’s Online Students?

At UofL Online, we welcome students with various academic and professional backgrounds. If you feel you were made for more, we are confident you will find your fit at UofL.

  • Transfer Students - choosing UofL to continue and finish their academic journey after attending another accredited higher education institution.
  • Returning Students - have earned previous college credit from UofL, coming back to school after a pause due to life or career demands.
  • First Time in College Students - no previous college credit, who cannot come to campus but want to earn a degree or certificate — maybe even for the first time in their family history.
  • Military-Connected Students - leveraging discipline and experience to pursue higher education while balancing service and family or preparing their transition to civilian life.
  • Internationally Located Students navigating education from afar, bringing global perspectives and seeking to advance their careers and future with a UofL degree.

Why is learning online beneficial for adult professionals 

  • Earning a degree online can help you fit education into your busy daily schedule and secure or advance in your current job, or even enter a new field or occupation.
  • While gaining knowledge in a specific subject or discipline, learning online can also help you develop transferable skills in time management, information organization, written communication or project management.
  • Many online programs can be accessed from any location, so there is no need to interrupt your professional and social routine to go to class.
  • Many classes are designed so you may attend any time that is convenient for you, there is no need to change your work schedule or put your career on hold.

How do you engage and collaborate in an online class

  • In an online class, you can get the one-on-one attention from your professors through user-friendly course platforms and direct communication (phone/email).
  • Online courses allow you to collaborate with colleagues on projects and assignments in real-time by using forums, discussion boards and other virtual tools.
  • Online students come from various backgrounds and locations. You can network and build professional relationships with classmates, regardless of your/their location (in the US or abroad).

What to expect while enrolled in online courses

  • Flexible courses, many offered in accelerated 5-week or 8-week terms
  • Interactive learning on Blackboard - UofL’s online learning platform
  • Weekly deadlines to keep you on track, with freedom to study anytime
  • Instructor communication, video lectures, digital resources and discussion boards
  • Group projects, presentations and feedback, all coordinated and managed online

What makes online students successful?

The most successful online students at UofL are working professionals with various work, life, community and family commitments, who are driven to reach their goal of earning their college degree. Many possess the following qualities:

Self-motivated

  • Online learning provides you with a great deal of flexibility; you can access your course when and where you want with little direct contact from your instructor. You must be motivated to keep up with coursework. If you get behind in the course, it will be very difficult to catch up.

Independent learners

  • Interactions between instructors and students in a traditional course are usually immediate. While your online course instructor will answer your questions and help with course material, it could take 24 hours or more to receive response to phone calls or emails. You must be comfortable learning in such an environment.

Computer literate

  • You don’t need to be an advanced computer user to succeed online, but it is helpful to have a basic knowledge of various computer applications, word processing software and the ability to send and receive e-mail. It is also helpful to have an understanding of how to conduct research on the Internet.

Good time managers

  • Since your course schedule will not revolve around regular face-to-face class sessions, you must be able to set a schedule that allows you to meet course deadlines. Depending on the course, you might also spend at least 3-5 hours per week on course-related activities. It takes good time management skills to balance classes with work, family and other obligations.

Effective communicators

  • Online courses require a great deal of reading and writing. You must be able to communicate thoughts, formulate questions or answers, and apply learned material through writing to other classmates and your instructor. Assignments and collaborative projects can help you develop your skills, learn from your instructors and peers, and tap into other resources as needed.

Effective problem solvers

  • You may face challenges in your online course that are outside of your control. For example, your computer or Internet connection may malfunction, your electricity may go out or you may have difficulty contacting your instructor. You must be able to think critically in such situations to determine the best course of action without becoming overly frustrated.

No Campus Classroom? No Problem. 
We Got You!

At UofL, your courses are designed to keep you connected, engaged and supported every step of the way. You’ll join a dynamic learning environment where professors know your name, classmates share your goals, and the tools you need to succeed are always within reach. 

Whether you’re logging in from your living room, your office or a café halfway around the world, you’ll find the same academic rigor, community and personal attention as you would on campus—delivered with the flexibility that fits your life.

Although you don’t need to be a tech wizard, you do need a few basics in order to thrive as an online student at UofL.


What you need to get started and learn online

  • A personal computer with internet access (high-speed recommended)
  • Basic software like Microsoft Office or Google Docs
  • A quiet space for study — at home, at a library, even in a break room at work
